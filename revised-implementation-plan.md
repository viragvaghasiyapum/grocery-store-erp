Phase 5 execution record (22 October 2026): **implemented and locally verified on production schema 33, including the single live direct-stock cutover.** Schema 32 makes completed sale values and posted returns immutable and caps returned quantity per sale line and refunds per sale at the original sale. Sales History derives Completed, Partially refunded, Refunded and Void from linked return documents through a per-sale refund-progress projection; no status flag changes an amount. A return keeps taxable, GST and cost proportions with final-return rounding, and spreads the bill round-off so a full return refunds exactly the grand total. Resellable units go back to the original lot only when its detail version and active state still match the sale. Otherwise a CUSTOMER_RETURN lot is created from the sale snapshot; a deleted product is recreated once under a new ID and conflict-safe SKU, and later returns reuse it. A legacy line missing required fields opens a prefilled review form instead of guessing. Units that are not resellable, or whose target lot has expired, become linked damage. Void is refused after any return or after a sold lot was deleted. Sales History filters invoice, date, product snapshot, status, payment mode and customer in SQL. The return dialog shows lot, expiry, sold, already returned, remaining, refund preview and stock destination; the detail activity lists completion, each partial return, full refund, damage and void. Lot details stay off the printed bill and reprints use the saved receipt. Hourly and per-product return projections and the category/GST report day, now keyed by the sale-time category snapshot, update incrementally.

Schema 33 switches every stock writer together. Purchase post and void, direct entry, sale completion and void, customer return, damage and its corrections, internal use, vendor return and cancellation, lot cost correction and the reviewed Products count each change lot quantity with one conditional update inside their document transaction. Legacy adjustments carry forward as immutable dated `stock_correction` rows and an unposted ledger count is closed. Phase 6 execution record (23 October 2026): **implemented and locally verified on production schema 34.** One P&L source contract, `v_pnl_entry`, feeds every live and finalized figure. Sales revenue excluding GST, bill round-off and COGS come from the sale-snapshot day projection. Returns reverse revenue, cost and their share of the round-off, so a full refund nets to zero. The other heads are ordinary and purchase Expense-line expenses (reversed by purchase void), damage net of recovery, stock corrections, salary in the month worked, depreciation, disposal gain or loss against the book value saved at disposal, and vendor-return variance. Internal-use stock, including its damage and vendor returns, has no automatic head; direct-origin cost enters only through sale COGS. Calendar and fiscal years lock independently from 8 January and 8 April by business date; the single `books.locked_until` setting is retired. A finalized year also stays locked if the clock is moved back. At startup, in idle maintenance and on the first request for a locked year, each locked year with P&L entries is finalized once. Its month/head/category lines, category names, edit cutoff, generation time, format version and SHA-256 checksum are saved; triggers make them immutable, and the checksum is verified on every read and weekly. Locked years, months inside them, and their CSV, Excel and A4 output read only that snapshot.

Posted expenses, damages and salary payments are never rewritten. Each Edit or Void is a dated correction with a before/after audit. While both annual periods containing the original date are open, the difference counts on that date; otherwise the original stays and the difference is dated on the business date. Moving an expense to another (open) date reverses the old value and applies the new one there; Void is a zeroing correction and a trigger blocks deletion. A damage quantity correction changes today's lot balance in the same transaction and recreates a deleted zero lot as a direct lot from the damage snapshot. Monthly salary is an effective-dated term, never earlier than the first month whose two years are both open. Posted payments stay as paid and the preview counts them. Asset method, rate and salvage are effective-dated: open posted months recalculate from the effective month, with a before/after depreciation and book-value preview and no recalculate-year option. Cost and in-use date can change only while every posted month is open, and are fixed once a finalized year's depreciation is posted. A never-posted month of a locked year is still posted for book-value continuity, with its charge dated in the current open period. These salary, internal-use, catch-up and asset-edit rules follow the four decisions the owner recorded at the start of the phase.

The current-unsold-stock report includes or excludes lots expired as of the business date and always states the expired lots, quantity and cost value. The purchased/direct-origin reconciliation shows each lot (newest first, or one product's lots) with received, sold, returned, damaged, internal-use, vendor-returned and corrected quantities, expected and available stock, and the difference. The full check of every lot reads all sale history, so it runs in weekly maintenance. That check records up to 200 differing lots in `lot_source_issue` and still fails maintenance so the difference is logged; **Differences only** rechecks just those lots. The same maintenance compares open-period P&L days with their sales and returns; `ScaleAudit rebuild-pnl-days` is the offline repair. Reports chooses Month, Calendar year, Fiscal year or Custom range and marks saved years "· Finalized". Its badge shows Open with the edit cutoff and as-of date, or Finalized with year, cutoff, version and checksum. Head totals come first, expense categories expand, months appear as columns, and the CSV is exactly the displayed version. Damages, Expenses and Posted payments open a narrow details panel with the dating statement, Edit/Void and history; it re-renders when the language changes. Assets shows **Effective from** with the recalculation preview and disables Save when blocked, and its timeline shows terms and charged-on dates.

Phase 7 execution record (24 October 2026): **implemented and locally verified on production schema 35.** The owner chose, at the start of the phase, a slide-in product details panel, one dialog per entry form, table pages sized to the window, and a compact one-line shell header. Navigation is Dashboard, Billing, Products, Purchases, Vendors, Sales History, Money out, Assets, Reports and Settings. The Stock page, its product stock and stock adjustment windows and the old catalog bulk editor are removed, Vendors has its own page, and the Money out expiry tab is gone; the expiry alert opens Products filtered to expired stock. Selecting a product opens a side panel with Basics, Lots and History tabs, and each tab's grid is created only when the tab is first selected. Entered values and focus survive a failed submit. The former inline Money out and Assets panels (expense, expense categories, repeat expenses, employee, salary run, advance, asset and disposal) are now dialogs, alongside the product, quick product, vendor, vendor payment, vendor return, direct lot, lot price, internal use, damage, correction, customer, return/void and legacy return-review forms. The legacy review asks Yes or No for each unsaved expiry or fractional-quantity setting instead of showing an undecided checkbox. Expense, salary and asset forms state before saving how their period treats the entry. In the purchase grid, Ctrl+D copies the cell above but never the line type, product or expense category; Ctrl+S saves the draft; and Post reports the new GRN.

Search, filtering, sorting and paging run in SQL with keyset cursors and ID tie-breakers. Each page's row count fits the visible grid (5–100 rows), and a non-empty product search returns eight. A newer request cancels the older one, so a stale result never replaces a newer one, and each page keeps its filters for the session. Products puts an exact SKU or barcode match first and then uses full-text/prefix matching. Its filters are type, status, category, has or low stock and expired/expiring; **More filters** adds brand, vendor, no barcode, missing local name and loose products. Lots filter by available/zero/blocked, batch, expiry bucket or date range, source and price range, and Products can list every product's expired lots without a separate Expiry screen. Purchases filter by GRN or vendor bill, vendor, status and dates, with line type, product and returnable stock under More filters. Sales History adds an amount range. Money out has Damages, Expenses, Employees and Posted payments tabs with date, status, category, reason and person filters, and Reports shows only the filters that apply to the chosen report. A UI test checks that every filter row stays one line high at 1366×768 with the navigation pane open. Migration 35 adds three indexes: a case-insensitive vendor-bill index on purchases, and partial name indexes holding only internal-use and archived products. On the full-size copy they cut a purchase document search from 171 ms to 0.07 ms and the internal-use/archived product views from 150–165 ms to under 0.01 ms, and they take about 640 KB.

Bulk selection distinguishes picked rows from "All N matching" and shows the exact count with the applied filters. The server resolves and rechecks the IDs (at most 5,000 products or lots, or 2,000 purchases or vendors). Before anything changes, a review lists eligible and skipped rows with reasons and the quantity/value effect. Eligible rows run in batches, each one transaction: 50 rows by default, 20 for an expired write-off and 5 for a purchase void, because a void reverses lots, vendor balance and expenses. Each batch's operation ID derives from a durable root ID, so a retried batch replays instead of posting twice. The operator can stop between batches and resume. The result shows changed, skipped, conflicted and pending counts and saves a CSV result list. The bulk actions are: product archive/restore, category, tax and type changes, labels (up to 200), barcodes, reconcile selected stock (up to 100), expired write-off, lot block/unblock, zero-lot deletion, selected-lot damage, purchase void and vendor archive/restore. No row has a generic quantity edit. A CSV export opens with the title, period and applied filters and closes with a totals row; Excel and A4 print the filters, and a capped export names the full-CSV alternative. Sixty-nine Gujarati strings that used લોટ now use બેચ (marked for review), and a localization test rejects લોટ.

## 1. Product contract and precedence

The decisions and acceptance criteria in this file supersede older statements in `revised plan.md`, `implementation.md`, `PLANS.md`, and supporting Phase 0 notes wherever they disagree. Preserve the offline, single-user Windows application, three-decimal fixed-point values, bilingual UI, durable transactions, backups, and existing payment, tax, and printing guarantees.

| Area | Final rule |
|---|---|
| Stock source | A lot's current quantity is authoritative. Stop creating generic stock-movement records and remove the stock ledger. Business documents still record their own quantities and costs so stock can be checked and recovered. |
| Product types | Saleable products can be sold. Internal-use products remain outside automatic P/L; the operator decides the cost of any later custom saleable product. |
| Purchase line type | Each line selects Product (default), Internal use, or Expense. A Product line creates a new saleable lot; an Internal-use line creates its own lot; an Expense line creates an expense entry and no saleable lot. Never merge receipts into an existing lot. |
| Direct entry | A product and its lot, or a later new lot, may be added without a purchase at any time. Its cost is saved on the lot and subsequently captured on a sale. Creating the lot itself does not count its cost twice in P/L. |
| Lots | One purchase line creates one distinct lot. FEFO is the default sale allocation. Staff may edit a lot's selling price and expiry. Quantity changes happen through sale, return, damage, purchase return, direct origin, internal use, or a reviewed stock correction workflow. A lot may be deleted only at zero quantity. |
| Products | A product with positive saleable stock cannot be deleted. Deleting an otherwise eligible product also deletes its zero-quantity lots. Historical documents carry their own snapshots and remain readable. |
| Completed sales | Sale lines, quantities, prices, tax, discounts, cost, and totals are final. Only allowed status transitions occur. Returns are separate documents. P/L uses sale snapshots and return documents, never current product or lot values. |
| Customer returns | Restock the original usable lot only if its version still matches the version saved on the sale. If it was edited or deleted, create a new return lot from saved sale details, at the booked cost and with the tax the unit was bought at (§15, T-2). If the product was deleted, the operator picks an active product of the same type whose unit takes the quantity, or creates one prefilled from the saved details (P3-F3). A non-resellable return becomes damage, never saleable stock. Lot details appear in saved history but not on the printed customer receipt. |
| Purchases | A posted purchase and its lines are final. An untouched purchase may be voided or partially returned. After any of its stock is used, void is blocked and only an eligible partial return is allowed. A purchase return is a separate document and may be cancelled with an atomic reversal. |
| P/L sources | Posted sale, return, expense, damage, payroll, asset, and applicable purchase-return records are not hard-deleted. Corrections and voids remain represented. Internal-use purchases do not automatically enter P/L. |
| Periods | Calendar year: 1 January–31 December, editable through 7 January, locked from 8 January. Fiscal year: 1 April–31 March, editable through 7 April, locked from 8 April. Apply both independently. Save final totals and head/category/month breakdowns for each locked year. |
| Corrections | Remove the “recalculate year” choice from assets, damages, expenses, and payroll. While both annual periods containing an original date are open, an edit can correct that original date and refresh open reports. Once either annual period is locked, retain the original and post a correction dated in an open current period. Asset and employee terms use effective dates for future calculations. |
| Reports | Keep monthly, calendar-year, and fiscal-year P/L; current unsold stock with include/exclude expired toggle; and a purchased/direct-origin versus sold, returned, damaged, internally used, vendor-returned, and available quantity reconciliation. |
| Tax and prices | Every selling price and MRP includes tax. Purchase lines, lots and sale lines save their tax label and per-piece tax when created; P/L keeps the booked cost. See §15 (agreed 25 October 2026, implemented on schema 39 on 26 October 2026). |

Definitions: “untouched purchase” means no effective quantity-consuming or other dependent document has ever used any lot from the purchase; current quantity equaling received quantity is insufficient. “Locked” is derived from business date, not from whether a scheduled close job happened to run. Seven-day buffer means the seven dates immediately after year end are included.

## 2. Invariants to preserve in every phase

1. A single user action that changes a business document, lot quantity, projections, audit record, and operation ID commits in one SQLite transaction or changes none of them. Use conditional updates to prevent negative stock and idempotency keys to prevent double posting after retries.
2. Store all business values as scaled integers, using the existing three-decimal model. A sale line owns immutable snapshots of product identity, unit, lot ID and version, batch/expiry, MRP, selling price, discounts, taxable amount, GST rate/amount, gross amount, unit cost, and extended cost. Reprinting uses the saved receipt snapshot.
3. A posted purchase line owns its vendor, product, tax, landed-cost, quantity, and lot-origin snapshots. No later product deletion or lot edit rewrites that document. A purchase line and its created lot have a one-to-one origin while the lot exists.
4. A deleted product or lot ID is never reused for a different item. Historical references may be nullable live links, but their snapshot IDs and descriptions remain stable. Any new product/lot created for a return or restore receives new IDs and a link to its source: a customer return's lot through `sale_return_line.restock_lot_id`, and a damage correction's restored lot through `damage_correction.restored_lot_id`. Recorded exception: a lot recreated when a vendor return is cancelled (P3-3, P3-F3) is a direct lot linked only by its reason, which names the return, and by the cancellation's audit entry, which lists the lots; the optional link table was not built.
5. Every lot has a recorded origin: posted purchase line, direct entry, or customer-return recreation. A current quantity must never arise from an unrecorded change. Dedicated source documents are allowed; a generic stock-movement table is not.
6. P/L sales and COGS use completed, partially returned, and fully returned sale snapshots, less linked return snapshots. A void sale contributes zero. A refund status alone never subtracts money or stock.
7. A closed annual report reads its saved snapshot. Open reports read current source records or bounded daily summaries. A later correction is dated in an open period; it cannot mutate a frozen annual snapshot.
8. Product and lot deletion, purchase void, and purchase-return cancellation check dependent documents, not only current lot balance. Full reconciliation checks source-document arithmetic; interactive writes update only affected lots and projections.
9. Keep existing backup, restore, print-job, period, and operation-ID safety. No UI write path may bypass the service transaction and database constraints.

## 3. Scale baseline and acceptance method

The current recorded full-size fixture has roughly 47,000 products, 222,500 lots, 870,000 sales, 5.19 million sale lines, and 5.41 million stock movements in a roughly 2.31 GB main database. A sustained local run measured sale-commit p95 at 27.4 ms, sampled WAL peak at 38.7 MB, and process working-set peak at 167.5 MB. A separate WPF journey peaked at about 327 MiB. These runs used uncontrolled OS cache and are baselines, not predictions of the revision. See docs/perf/phase6-results.md.

Use a preserved pre-migration fixture plus a migrated disposable copy. Add adversarial fixtures: many lots per hot product, identical batch/expiry across separate receipts, partially returned purchases, refunded sales, deleted products followed by returns, expired lots, direct lots, internal-use lines, and year-boundary corrections. Record machine, database size, free disk, row counts, WAL size, working set, GC allocations, write duration, UI first-frame callback, and query plans. Compare before/after on the same hardware and workload; repeat fresh-process and warm measurements. A target-shop-PC pilot remains a separate gate.

Candidate gates, subject to Phase 0 confirmation on target hardware:

| Workload | Gate |
|---|---|
| Scan to bill line / product search to visible result / bill commit | Retain existing functional targets: under 50 ms / under 100 ms / under 100 ms, respectively, using p95 for the measured workload. Distinguish app callback from physical screen paint. |
| Search SQL | p95 under 30 ms for an eight-result search page on the full fixture. Empty, broad, no-match, Gujarati, SKU, and barcode shapes must all be measured. |
| Normal WPF navigation | No unbounded page-load queries or retained page collections; repeated full navigation must not show rising steady memory. Candidate full-size peak at or below 350 MiB on the reference PC, with no regression on the target PC. |
| Sustained writes plus reads and backup | Zero errors or quantity/report mismatches; commit p95 under 100 ms, sampled WAL below 64 MiB, and process working set no higher than the same-machine baseline without an explained reason. |
| Full-history CSV | Streaming and cancellation with memory proportional to the page size, not the number of rows; target a peak below 512 MiB for the five-million-row run, improving the recorded 705 MiB pass. |
| Storage and backup | Measure table/index bytes with SQLite dbstat before and after compaction; report actual savings. Keep the existing approximately 1 GB verified-backup-under-60-second target on the target PC. |

Do not treat a faster isolated SQL query as acceptance if it shifts work to sale commits, startup, backup, WPF layout, or memory. Preserve p50/p95/p99 and row/result parity for every changed hot path.

### 15.2 Definitions and worked example

For one purchase line, where *units* = quantity + free quantity:

- **Purchase price** = (quantity × rate − discount + the line's share of other charges) ÷ units.
- **Purchase price incl. tax** = (quantity × rate − discount + share of other charges + line tax) ÷ units.
- **Tax/unit** = Purchase price incl. tax − Purchase price, so the three saved values add up exactly at three decimals.
- **Booked cost** (`purchase_line.landed_unit_cost`, `product_lot.unit_cost`, `sale_line.unit_cost`) = Purchase price in Regular mode and Purchase price incl. tax otherwise. These are the values the app books today.
- Tax is charged on the goods (quantity × rate − discount). **Other charges** are spread across the bill's lines by value, taken exactly as typed, and get no tax; if the transporter charged GST, the operator types the charge including it.

| Example line | Amount |
|---|---|
| 10 pieces at ₹100, less ₹50 discount | ₹950.00 |
| GST 5% on ₹950 | ₹47.50 |
| Share of ₹60 freight on a two-line bill | ₹30.00 |
| Paid for the line | ₹1,027.50 for 10 + 2 free = 12 pieces |
| Purchase price | 980 ÷ 12 = 81.667 |
| Purchase price incl. tax | 1,027.50 ÷ 12 = 85.625 |
| Tax/unit | 85.625 − 81.667 = 3.958 |

On screen this reads 81.67 + 3.96 = 85.63. Multiplying 81.67 by 1.05 gives 85.75, which is wrong because it taxes the freight.

Display rules:

- Money shows two decimals in every money column of the tables §15.3 changes, so a row reads consistently. In a row that shows A + B = C, B is displayed as the displayed C minus the displayed A, so the row adds up on screen; saved three-decimal values and every report are unchanged.
- The label is the tax rate's name at the time the record was saved. A sale line on which no tax is charged (Composition or No-GST mode) shows "—". If a sale line's tax rate no longer has the percent the line was charged at, the label is built from the line's own percent ("Tax 5%"), so a label never contradicts the tax charged.
- Records saved before schema 39, and lots whose new values are empty, show "—" in the new columns.

## 16. Screen help

Status: **agreed with the owner on 6 November 2026 and built on branch `screen-help`, first for the release after 1.2.0. On 8 November 2026 the owner chose to ship it in 1.2.0, with the totals footer and the calculation audit's fixes, so the 1.2.0 packages and their gates are redone** (`docs/release-checklist.md`). Execution record at the top.

### 16.1 Decisions

- Every page and dialog gets an ⓘ button beside its title, or beside its buttons when it has no title. Hovering shows what the screen is for; clicking it, or pressing F1 anywhere in the screen, opens a help window: what the screen is for, then each filter, field, column and button, and how to use the screen.
- F1 in a field opens the help with that field's row marked and scrolled into view. There are no new tooltips on single fields; the screens keep the ones they have.
- The help text is one Markdown file per screen and language, `docs/user-guide/help/{en,gu}/{topic}.md`, embedded in the app. The same files are the user guide's screen pages, so a screen's help and its guide page cannot differ. This is the one exception to PLANS.md §18's rule that application text lives in `.resx`; tests apply the same parity rules.
- The help follows the interface language. The Gujarati help is machine-drafted and marked `MT – needs review` until the native-Gujarati review.
- No new package: `HelpDocument` reads a small part of Markdown (a title, a summary, `##` sections, paragraphs, lists and two-column tables) and leaves out images, link targets and comments, so a guide page can show its screenshot.

### 16.2 How it works

- `ScreenHelp.Topic`, an inherited attached property on the root of a page or dialog, names the topic. The `HelpButton` beside the title takes the topic from there. A window built in code, or one window that serves two purposes, sets it with `ScreenHelp.SetTopic`.
- `ScreenHelp.Register`, called at startup, binds WPF's Help command, whose key is F1, on every window. It opens the topic of the focused element; outside a screen with a topic, F1 does nothing.
- The focused field's label is a table cell's column header, else the nearest accessible name, button text, or menu or tab header. The help marks every table row whose bold name is that label (case, spaces and a trailing ▾, …, : or * do not matter) and scrolls the first one to the top.
- A screen with tabs has a section per tab, headed with the tab's name or with the name, a colon and more ("Lots: filters"). The ⓘ, or F1 outside any field, opens the help at the first section of the open tab of a visible tab control; F1 in a field inside a tab marks that field's rows in the tab's sections, or every row naming it when the tab's sections have none.
- `HelpWindow` is modal and never taller than the work area. The help scrolls, and it has focus, so the arrow and page keys scroll it; Enter or Esc closes it.
- A title row whose last actions move under More ▾ (`ActionStrip.TitleRow`) must count the ⓘ in the title's width.

### 16.3 Tests

- `ScreenHelpTests`: every topic a view names, in XAML or with `ScreenHelp.SetTopic` in code, has an English and a Gujarati file, and every file belongs to a topic. Both files have the same sections, tables, rows and list items. Every bold name in a table's first column is a label the app shows in that language (a resource string or a literal XAML label), so renaming a label fails until its help follows. English digits only, and no લોટ. The reader keeps to its subset and refuses anything else.
- `Shell_LoadsAndSwitchesToGujarati`: the ⓘ and F1 on each screen with help, a help row for each of its filters and columns in both languages (`VerifyPageHelp`), the ⓘ and a row for each form field and column of every dialog it shows (`VerifyDialogHelp`), and the help window at an F1 field and at an open tab within a 600-pixel work area.
